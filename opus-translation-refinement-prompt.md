# Translation Refinement Prompt for Opus

## Project Context

**Book:** Theory of Castes and Roles (Теория Каст и Ролей)
**Genre:** Non-fiction, motivation, personal development, social philosophy
**Original Language:** Russian
**Target Language:** American English
**Author:** Alexey Krol

## Background

The book has two existing translations:
1. **Old human translation** (~8 years old) - more natural-sounding, conversational American English, but translated from an earlier version of the book
2. **New translation** (recent) - more accurate and precise, preserves philosophical depth, but sometimes sounds formal or "translated"

## Task

**Primary Objective:** Refine the new translation to make it sound more natural and native for American readers while preserving accuracy and intellectual depth.

**Core Principle:** Depth comes from clarity of thought, not from heavy language.

## Target Audience

- **Primary:** Educated professionals feeling stuck in their careers/lives
- **Demographics:** People experiencing income stagnation, social role stagnation, frustration with their situation
- **Reading level:** College-educated, intellectually curious
- **Expectations:** Serious content delivered in accessible, clear language

## Stylistic References

Your tone should resemble these successful American non-fiction authors:

1. **Malcolm Gladwell** - Conversational but intelligent, storytelling approach to complex ideas
2. **Daniel Kahneman** (*Thinking, Fast and Slow*) - Serious content, crystal-clear language, no unnecessary complexity
3. **Yuval Noah Harari** (*Sapiens*) - Profound ideas expressed simply, engaging direct address to reader
4. **Cal Newport** - Straightforward, clear, precise without being academic

**Common thread:** All write for educated audiences using clear, accessible language. They don't artificially complicate; they clarify.

## Editing Principles

### 1. Clarity Over Complexity
- Simplify sentence structure where possible without losing meaning
- Break long sentences into shorter ones when it improves readability
- Use active voice unless passive serves a specific purpose

### 2. Natural American English
- Replace formal constructions with natural-sounding alternatives
- Use contractions where appropriate (but not excessively)
- Prefer common words over rare ones when meaning is identical
- Ensure idioms and expressions sound native, not translated

### 3. Conversational But Intelligent Tone
- Write as if speaking to a smart friend, not lecturing to students
- Direct address to reader ("you") should feel personal, not academic
- Maintain authority without stuffiness
- Balance accessibility with respect for reader's intelligence

### 4. Preserve Precision
- **Never simplify ideas themselves** - only simplify their expression
- Keep technical terms when they're the most accurate
- Maintain philosophical depth and nuance
- Don't "dumb down" - clarify

### 5. Rhythm and Flow
- Read aloud test: Would this sound natural if spoken?
- Vary sentence length for rhythm
- Use punctuation to create natural pauses
- Ensure smooth transitions between ideas

## Specific Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- **Avoid:** "It is worth noting that..." → **Use:** "Keep in mind..."
- **Avoid:** "In order to achieve..." → **Use:** "To achieve..."
- **Avoid:** "There is a necessity to..." → **Use:** "You must..." or "You need to..."

### Word Choice
- **Formal:** "ascertain" → **Natural:** "figure out" or "determine"
- **Formal:** "commence" → **Natural:** "start" or "begin"
- **Formal:** "endeavor" → **Natural:** "try" or "effort"
- **Keep when precise:** "caste," "paradigm," "jurisdiction" (technical terms)

### Tone Examples
- **Too formal:** "One must consider the ramifications of..."
- **Better:** "Consider what happens when..."
- **Too casual:** "You gotta think about..."
- **Right balance:** "You need to think about..."

## Examples: Before and After

### Example 1: Opening
**Current (formal):**
> "Over the past fifty years, the world has become incredibly complex. Simplified meanings of life have faded away, non-obvious principles of social advancement have started to operate, and society has split into countless groups at war with one another."

**Refined (natural):**
> "Over the past fifty years, the world has become incredibly complex. Simple answers about life's meaning have disappeared. Society has fractured into countless warring groups, each operating by unspoken rules of advancement that aren't obvious to outsiders."

### Example 2: Values and Worldview
**Current (formal):**
> "Values and worldview do not reflect reality; they only provide notions about it. Those notions rely less on facts and more on faith and convictions."

**Refined (natural):**
> "Your values and worldview don't reflect reality—they reflect your beliefs about reality. And those beliefs rely less on facts than on faith and conviction."

### Example 3: Call to Action
**Current (formal):**
> "Thus, we have to get to the bottom of the meaning of life. This is important at least because, as a result, it will be very hard to throw you off."

**Refined (natural):**
> "This is why we need to get to the bottom of life's meaning. Once you know what you want and why you're living, it becomes nearly impossible for others to manipulate you."

### Example 4: Technical Content
**Current (formal):**
> "Resource scarcity has formed the dominant value system of the inhabitants of planet Earth."

**Refined (natural):**
> "Resource scarcity has shaped the dominant value system on Earth."

*(Note: Keep "planet Earth" only if it serves a rhetorical purpose; otherwise simplify)*

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Does it sound natural when read aloud?
- [ ] Is every idea as clear as possible without being simplified?
- [ ] Have I removed unnecessary formality without losing authority?
- [ ] Are all idioms and expressions native-sounding?
- [ ] Does the tone balance accessibility with intellectual seriousness?
- [ ] Have I preserved all conceptual precision and philosophical depth?
- [ ] Would an educated American reader find this engaging rather than academic?

## Common Patterns to Fix

### Pattern 1: Passive Constructions
- **Change:** "It is important to understand..." → "You need to understand..."
- **Change:** "This can be observed in..." → "You can see this in..."

### Pattern 2: Wordy Phrases
- **Change:** "In order to" → "To"
- **Change:** "It is necessary to" → "You must" or "You need to"
- **Change:** "For the purpose of" → "To"
- **Change:** "With regard to" → "About" or "Regarding"

### Pattern 3: Abstract Subjects
- **Change:** "The situation demands..." → "You must..." or "This requires..."
- **Change:** "Circumstances require..." → "You need to..."

### Pattern 4: Overly Formal Transitions
- **Change:** "Thus, it follows that..." → "So..." or "Therefore..."
- **Change:** "Consequently..." → "As a result..." or "This means..."
- **Change:** "Nevertheless..." → "But..." or "Yet..." or "Still..."

1. Read the entire chapter first to understand context and flow
2. Edit section by section, maintaining consistency
3. Pay special attention to:
   - Opening paragraphs (set the tone)
   - Transitions between sections
   - Key conceptual explanations
   - Direct addresses to the reader
4. After editing, review the full chapter for flow and consistency
5. Save edited version with clear naming (e.g., `CH-01.en-US.refined.md`)

## Important Notes

- **Preserve all markdown formatting** (headers, lists, emphasis)
- **Keep all technical terms** related to the caste system
- **Maintain the author's voice** - we're polishing, not rewriting
- **Don't over-edit** - if a sentence is already natural, leave it alone
- **When in doubt, prioritize clarity** over any other consideration

## Success Criteria

The refined translation should:
1. Sound like it was originally written in American English
2. Be accessible to educated readers without specialized background
3. Maintain all philosophical depth and conceptual precision
4. Flow naturally from sentence to sentence, paragraph to paragraph
5. Engage the reader rather than lecture them
6. Feel like a conversation with a knowledgeable friend, not a textbook
