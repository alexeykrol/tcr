# CH-11 -- HOW DIFFERENT ROLES AFFECT FATE

How Different Roles Affect Fate
Fate, in our terminology, is only a trajectory in the matrix of castes and roles, and a comparative analysis of these fate-trajectories makes it possible to identify certain patterns.
- Not all roles provide the same opportunities for growth. There are roles that stimulate development and the achievement of certain goals, and there are roles that practically do not influence growth and sometimes even hinder it.
- There are roles that help you secure yourself in the upper castes, and there are roles that, by virtue of their characteristics, increase the risk of getting thrown out of the upper caste.
- A growth strategy is not connected with your profession. It is connected with the ability to see business opportunities, attract partners, organize and develop your own venture.
- There is an optimal time window when role-based growth potential is at its maximum. In youth you still do not have the traits that would allow you to master the necessary roles, and in maturity most people's energy level drops, the level needed for the Entrepreneur role, for example. Don't waste time.
- The main growth potential lies in the combination of three roles: Dealmaker, Manager, and Entrepreneur. Their presence is enough to build from scratch almost any project and achieve a high financial status. Hundreds of thousands of people go this way, unburdened by education or attachment to any profession. For them the main thing is a stable source of income.
- The weakest roles in terms of growth potential are Specialist, Teacher, and Doer.
- To scale your business, to turn it from small to large, you need to master the roles of Financier, Politician, and Leader.
- You can make someone a Financier or an Official, but it is very hard to make them a Politician and almost impossible to make them a Leader if they are not predisposed to it.
The fates of the Active Entrepreneur and the Talented Loser are telling. They reached the upper castes but could not stay there. The reason is that they lacked the strategic skills provided by roles with high growth potential. And at the same time, our heroes were hindered by roles that tied them to an outdated value system and therefore did not allow them to move forward.
The analysis we conducted illustrates a simple thought: at the foundation of the success that determines fate lies the answer not to the question "What should I do?" but to the question "What roles should I play?" This is a fundamental difference, because the overwhelming majority of people ask themselves the wrong questions and therefore get the wrong answers that lead nowhere.
The most common questions sound like this:
- What should I do?
- What field should I go into?
- What kind of thing should I do?
- What topic should I start developing?
The trouble is that asking these questions makes no sense as long as you remain who you are. Because the answers will take you nowhere. Yet people spend time searching for them.
The right question sounds like this: "What kind of person do I need to become in order to:
- see a new opportunity, topic, innovation;
- find people who see such opportunities and can tell me about them;
- attract people who will help me realize these new opportunities;
- attract partners who will supply me with resources;
- find doers?"
People who are looking for a specific thing (a profession, a business) ask for a fish. The one who is busy with inner transformation looks for a net to catch fish in unlimited quantities. The concept of castes and roles gives us that net and answers the question: "How do I need to change, transform, what qualities do I need in order to develop and grow socially?"
Ideal Fates
What is an ideal fate, from the point of view of [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC)? One that implies the purposeful replacement of ineffective roles with effective ones. And as a consequence--systematic movement from lower castes to higher ones. I propose analyzing a couple of ideal fates and seeing how they are reflected on the matrix of castes and roles.
The Ideal Fate of an Oligarch and a Businessman
The future Oligarch started as a Doer, got an education, and worked as a Specialist to understand a specific industry. Pretty quickly he mastered the Dealmaker role and learned the art of self-presentation. Having achieved certain successes, he moved into management. He began to show initiative in different directions and soon mastered the Entrepreneur role: he noticed an interesting business theme, attracted partners, and started his venture. He focused on income, profit, and management. All applied questions he assigned to Specialists.
As the business grew, he began to pay more attention to communication, strategy, attracting the best Managers and Specialists. He mastered the Financier role and focused on searching for the right circle of strategic partners. He found several interesting assets and bought them with borrowed funds. He began mastering the roles of Official and Politician in order to end up in the right circle. The right circle is access to personnel, larger-scale resources, new horizons. He expanded his business, buying everything with growth potential. He began separating flies from cutlets: he strengthened core businesses and sold non-core ones. His focus shifted more and more from operational business management into the realm of searching for and acquiring assets with good growth potential.
Big advantages require closer relations with power. He went into politics and public organizations, made connections, gained access to government projects. His innate entrepreneurial talent allowed him to see opportunities and attract people who can notice prospects and strive for self-realization. He manifested the Leader role more and more. Those who saw in him opportunities for their own advancement began to gravitate toward him. He united innovators and investors, creating large social business platforms. Rulers began to consult with him, Siloviks to flirt, and competitors to respect. Our hero always remained constructive and positive. Everyone wanted and wants to deal with him. He himself is constantly in search of new megaprojects.
The Ideal Fate of a Creative Professional
In my experience, many young people, looking at the "ideal fate" of a Businessman or an Oligarch, say: "No, I don't want that. My calling is to do creative work, become a TV host, a scientist, maybe an architect, an artist, a designer, a photographer..." And then down the list of glam professions. I understand: like most people, they are attracted not by the activity itself, but by the public and financial attributes of success that accompany it. I understand, but I do not share it, because the business path leads to these attributes far more often than the path of a creative professional. But young idiots don't believe it. They stubbornly keep dreaming about the footlights and the audience's ovations. So let's show the "ideal fate" of a creative person.
Suppose our hero mastered a creative, so-called free profession--for example, a singer. He became a good Specialist, then found a sponsor or a producer, realizing his potential in the Dealmaker role. He entered the caste of Professionals, built mastery, created a reputation, built connections. Gradually he abandoned the producer's services by developing the Entrepreneur role, hired his own support crew and an Executive Producer. He began producing young performers, investing money in them and taking the rights to the works they created, which he released under his label. Having reached decent turnover, he began investing funds into retail-and-leisure properties and built a distribution company.
He began promoting the children of wealthy people and gained access to serious financing. This pushed him to buy effective companies connected with the entertainment world, plus designer boutiques, retail chains, alcohol brands. In parallel he created a full-cycle studio that provided the entire spectrum of services: from producing songs and music videos to organizing tours. He launched a restaurant chain and produced several shows. As a result, he created a multi-industry vertically integrated holding operating in entertainment and adjacent segments. He began building hotels and theme parks, bringing in familiar stars for advertising. He produced film and TV projects, bought a stake in an entertainment TV network and several print and internet publications.
The Fate of a Creative Professional
I can draw many more successful and unsuccessful fates, but all of them will have similar patterns. And the main one is this: the faster a person switches from the Specialist role to other, more promising roles, the faster they begin to grow. In essence, a professional base is needed only so that you can then master the roles of Dealmaker, Manager, and Entrepreneur.
This needs to be done because:
- the Dealmaker role can immediately move you into the caste of Professionals;
- the Manager role will make it possible to realize that a team will always bring you more than working alone;
- the Entrepreneur role will help you see business opportunities and push you to create your own venture.
Producer Is a Multifunctional Role
Producer is a role that has been given undeservedly little attention on the pages of this book. To fix that, I propose that as a curtain call of [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC), we devote a few pages to analyzing it.
Today, when content occupies an increasingly significant place in the global economy, people who have mastered the Producer role face grand opportunities. The ability to generate and monetize ideas, while involving in their realization both representatives of the creative guild and business, government bodies, charitable organizations, the media, and opinion leaders, opens doors to the upper castes.
In this respect, the Producer role stands apart. Unlike the others, it makes it possible to make a leap in the hierarchy in the shortest time by initiating a project that will attract representatives of the castes you need. And only you will determine how to use this: build strong relationships with the powers that be, or, upon completion of the project, safely return to habitual existence.
The Producer role is a kind of little golden key capable of opening a secret door into a magical country, but not guaranteeing that you will remain there. The unique opportunities of this role are determined by its multifunctionality. It combines both the ability to generate ideas and the ability to understand who might benefit from implementing them, and the skill of constructing a project framework, creating a system.
This role is extremely difficult to master by sheer will. Its presence in your arsenal is largely determined by inborn talents and the environment that raised you. People who virtuously perform this role are highly valued by representatives of very different strata. However, by itself it is not very effective. The Producer role begins to bear fruit only if you have the Dealmaker and Manager roles. If we are talking about implementing not so much creative or charitable projects as business projects, it is important that you also have the Entrepreneur and Financier roles. Otherwise all your initiatives will smell strongly of dreamy, Manilov-style nonsense.
The Delusions of Creative People
I am often asked (especially by representatives of creative professions): "How can you achieve success while focusing only on your craft?" The answer is: no way. What does "focusing on your craft" mean? Remaining in the Specialist role your whole life. Remaining a Specialist, you will not achieve success in any way. Your lot is the caste of Slaves. Period.
After my answer, as a rule, an argument begins. People start citing successful writers as examples. In particular, J. K. Rowling. Sometimes painters, but there we usually quickly find out that for the most part their canvases started selling only after their death, and the person themselves died in poverty. And yet the myth of the rich creator (most often, yes, a writer) is fed by something. Many of us continue to believe that somewhere there are lucky ones who do nothing except their creative work and feel great. So under certain circumstances, is the Specialist role enough for prosperity? The answer is no. The story of any rich creator is like an iceberg: everyone sees only the tip.
In reality, to truly earn money from your creative work, you need to be at least a good Dealmaker. Because the fruits of creativity must be sold profitably in a highly competitive market. Rowling had enough skill to get an unconventional-at-the-time fairy tale published. And then she had enough grit to secure for herself the right to approve the casting when choosing actors for the Harry Potter film. By comparison, none of the well-known Russian writers managed to do that with regard to screen adaptations of their works. That is, most likely we are dealing with a very well-developed Dealmaker role. And I suspect also Financier, since Rowling has managed to preserve her fortune for so long.
Of course, there is always room for a miracle. You can get incredibly lucky and meet a good agent on your path, one who turns out to be an excellent Dealmaker, Producer, Entrepreneur, Politician, and Financier all in one person. Perhaps also your husband or wife. But that kind of luck is outside the scope of this book.
If we do not indulge in fantasies, then we are faced with a mercilessly categorical fact: not a single creative person is capable of succeeding while doing exclusively creative work. Because in this world, they are not the only one competing for the public's attention. Just think: on Earth, only two percent of writers earn enough from books to live on royalties alone. In Hollywood, out of 130,000 scripts submitted annually, only 2,500 are filmed.
You don't like that statistic? You have another choice: publish the book yourself, organize the exhibition, produce the musical work, film, or stage production yourself. You will fully control the situation--welcome to the world of Dealmakers, Entrepreneurs, and Managers. You don't like it when you are unable to control your fate? Welcome to the society of those who learn to create their fate, their worlds. And [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC) is only one of the tools to wake you up and offer you to take responsibility for your life.
Conclusion
According to a common myth, for every person in this world there are many paths leading to success. Alas, it is an illusion. The choice of roads exists only for the one who is the master of their fate. The one who does not belong to themselves has only one path--the path of a slave.
This means that if you analyze all life trajectories laid out on the matrix of castes and roles, you will discover astonishing monotony. Whatever professions people choose, whatever families they are born into, whatever cataclysms happen to them, all their fates can be divided into two types: successful and unsuccessful. Nothing else.
A successful fate consists of the right sequence of the roles of Specialist, Dealmaker, Manager, Entrepreneur, Financier, and Politician. An unsuccessful fate means that you have only one or several roles: Specialist; or Specialist and Dealmaker; or Dealmaker and Entrepreneur. Any incomplete combination of the fundamental roles means an unsuccessful fate, even if you are a very smart person. Even if you are a genius.
But if the Dealmaker and Entrepreneur roles have a chance to lead you to at least relative well-being, the Specialist role without support from other roles leads to a one-hundred-percent collapse. Because it is the only one of all of them aimed at interacting with material objects or the fruits of intellectual effort. That is, not at relationships with people. And this is one of the main traps of the world of castes and roles. A trap that everyone who has read this book is simply obligated to avoid.
