# CH-10 -- SUCCESSFUL BUSINESSMAN

Successful Businessman
This is how the fate of the Successful Businessman took shape. He got into college, but studying did not attract him. He copied off his buddies and did not consider it shameful. But his business instinct showed itself immediately: he was the commander of a student construction brigade and started doing side work on construction sites. Money appeared, and connections with useful people formed. He asked to become an assistant to one crafty underground shop boss. The guy sewed fur coats and moved them. The young man helped deliver the goods to retail outlets, without giving up his construction side job. After some time, there was an offer to work on a serious site. The construction site lacked materials--our hero managed to get them at a good price and sell them to his employers. The fur-coat school helped.
After graduating, he was drafted into the army. He served the required term as an officer, since his college had a military department, and he returned to the business that had been frozen for a time. Gradually he created his own construction company. He fought for small contracts. Talked with builders and architects. Then he realized it was more profitable to build his own projects than to work for someone else. Soon he found a developer who had land but did not have their own construction company. He proposed a partnership: our hero builds cheaper, but the developer gives him part of the profit in apartments. It was profitable for everyone. When they finished the project, the future Successful Businessman had already sold all his apartments, while the developer could not sell even half.
The Successful Businessman offered to sell the developer's apartments for a good commission. The developer agreed, since he needed money to buy the next plot of land. Gradually the partnership came to this: the developer looked for land and a project, and our hero built and sold. Relationships with financiers emerged; he understood how to attract money. Soon smart people taught him: to get land, you need connections with power, ideas, and money. Our hero already had a name, he knew how to attract money, and ideas were supplied by the people working for him. Very quickly he acquired the needed connections. The partner was no longer needed.
When the Successful Businessman realized that the key is land and money, he bought a small bank. Now he not only bought land, devised and built projects, but also provided financing for himself and other construction companies. Gradually the entire chain ended up in his hands: from idea to implementation, including the purchase of land and assets, construction financing, and so on. Fifteen years after his first experience in the student construction brigade, the Successful Businessman managed to create a holding company that included a development company, a construction complex, suppliers of various materials, a specialized recruiting agency, a network real-estate brokerage, investment and universal banks. His business had a capitalization of more than $5 billion and was growing dynamically. Our hero became chairman of the board and, in essence, the monopoly owner.
From the point of view of [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC): from youth the Successful Businessman knew how to sell and was oriented toward money, while his social growth became possible when he complemented the Dealmaker role with the roles of Manager (working in the construction brigade) and Entrepreneur (communicating with the fur-coat trader). The Specialist role was practically absent in him, so he did not burden himself with attachment to a specific craft. As soon as the key roles of Dealmaker, Entrepreneur, and Manager activated, he made a leap: he created his own construction firm. It is very important that precisely these three roles coexisted in him, because often people have an entrepreneurial streak, but the Dealmaker or Manager role is alien to them. Which is why they cannot create a viable business.
A qualitative breakthrough in business--from a modest construction company to a full-fledged holding, from making money to capitalization--the Successful Businessman made by mastering the Financier role. And finally, the next leap--into the caste of Oligarchs--he made thanks to the Politician role.
The path of the Successful Businessman: (1–12T)>(2–12M)>(3–12E)>(4–9E)>(5–9F)>(6–3F)>(7–3P)>(8–2P).
Active Entrepreneur
The Active Entrepreneur was not very attracted to education. He served in the army, where he mastered the trade of an auto mechanic. When he returned to civilian life, acquaintances suggested bringing in cars to sell. Since he was a good driver and not the timid type, he agreed. It took off. As a result, not a single day after the army was our hero a salaried employee; he immediately worked only for himself. Very quickly he began to show healthy pragmatism. When he bought cars for resale, he also purchased the most scarce spare parts. After selling the cars, he sold surplus parts to familiar service shops. After some time, he was asked to bring in a truck, and he decided not to drive it empty--he bought spare parts and scarce goods. That is how the Active Entrepreneur started a business connected with selling spare parts.
At first he offered the goods to auto repair shops, but later opened a couple of spots at wholesale markets. The decision turned out to be successful, because competition in bringing in import cars was high, while competition in the spare-parts trade was not. This business produced a good margin and was considered relatively calm. Therefore, soon the Active Entrepreneur became the owner of a small network of spare-parts stores and service stations. The business brought him money that allowed him to buy good cars for personal use and acquire several apartments. But the work took too much time, since the Active Entrepreneur preferred to perform many key tasks himself, not trusting assistants.
From the point of view of [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC), we have a typical Specialist with an entrepreneurial streak, which automatically places him in the caste of Professionals. The need not only to bring in but also to buy and sell cars developed the Dealmaker role in him, which, in turn, made it possible to build a relatively stable business. At the same time, the Manager role was weak in him. It allowed him to organize an additional business, but the Specialist prevailed, not allowing him to delegate truly serious tasks. As a result, he was forced to spend a lot of time on secondary operations, which he considered key.
Lack of time and concentration on routine did not make it possible to adequately assess threats and prospects. The business fed him, but had low growth potential, since all the attention of the Active Entrepreneur was directed more toward management than toward development. In the end our hero sank deeper and deeper into everyday churn, not trying to look around at what was happening. At the same time, there was no one to borrow innovative ideas for developing the business from--the Active Entrepreneur was surrounded exclusively by working stiffs. He himself thought tactically, lacking the resources to switch to strategy.
Gradually, large competitors forced him to shut the business down, and he got hired by a company that sold spare parts and owned service stations. When the time came, he moved to the countryside, received a pension, worked a garden, and also did small services for neighbors. His main income came from renting out apartments.
The path of the Active Entrepreneur: (1–12W)>(2–12T)>(3–12E)>(4–8E)>(5–7E)>(6–8M)>(7–10M)>(8–7W).
Successful Creator
The Successful Creator went to an arts university and became a director. Breaking through was not easy, but he was determined. At first he took on any job: shot low-budget commercials, wrote scripts. Gradually he built a name and began to receive serious orders: large-scale advertising for major companies, music videos, corporate films. But he was always drawn to big cinema, so he began to focus exclusively on expensive work, rejecting other offers. He was talented, persistent, and always constructive. Demand for his services grew, which allowed him to raise his price. The Successful Creator had many producer acquaintances, and one of them suggested creating a film company under his name. He agreed on the condition that he would mostly do creative work, not touching the administrative process. At the same time, he demanded the maximum possible share of profits, since he understood that clients wanted to work specifically with him.
From that moment on, all contracts were concluded with his film company, where he acted as the main shareholder. Gradually there were more orders, so he began to pass them on to young directors he trusted. Shooting a large number of music videos ensured connections among stars and sponsors. He became a member of the glam crowd, though he did not spend much time on it. When he was offered to direct a feature film, the Successful Creator gladly agreed, since the script interested him. During filming, financing was suspended, and he had to turn to one of his many friends. Our hero was not refused, but he was asked many questions about the film business. The need to answer forced the Successful Creator to dig deeper into the process. After figuring it out, he realized that financially it was much more profitable to be a producer than a director.
From that moment on, he went into producing, using his connections to finance film projects. Since the volume of work noticeably grew, he was forced to hire professional financiers and lawyers. They were expensive specialists, but they were worth it. The CFO proved that with such a production volume, ordering services from outside production companies was not profitable. It was far more cost-effective to have your own production base. They found money for its creation without problems--from the same oligarch acquaintances, who asked in exchange for a minor stake.
Now the film company could not only make its own films, but also provide services to outside producers. The bottleneck turned out to be studio space, and our hero began looking for it to rent. At that moment a businessman acquaintance offered him to buy a sound stage by taking a loan. The loan amount turned out to be comparable to rental payments, and our hero agreed. A few years later the value of the asset grew several times, and he managed first to refinance it and then, profitably selling illiquid space, to fully pay off the loan. His studio ran without interruption, producing feature films for media platforms, series, corporate films. One distributor acquaintance proposed merging their companies, creating a vertically integrated holding. Our hero agreed on the condition that he would own a majority stake. He also agreed that if he attracted money for major projects, he would receive preferential terms.
The capitalization of the combined company increased; the market noticed it. It still worked effectively, and the Successful Creator continued making films, running the business through qualified and loyal managers. After buying the rights to several failed films, the company incurred losses, and the Successful Creator parted ways with his partner. After some time, he met with the head of a major distributor and discussed the possibility of a merger with a division of responsibility on the following terms: our hero handles production and producing, and his partner handles distribution. To expand the production base, they created a film-town project with a modern studio, film park, and a complex of residential and commercial real estate. A favorable real-estate price, a name, and good connections made it possible to attract the needed investors. After five years of construction and the launch of the site, the capitalization of the combined media business amounted to more than $10 billion. The Successful Creator continued occasionally making movies, produced projects he found interesting, and owned a stake in a huge entertainment media holding.
From the point of view of [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC): mastering the directing profession allowed him to develop two roles at once: Specialist and Manager. Work in the advertising business and in the professional market developed skills inherent in the Dealmaker, which, in turn, made it possible to quickly move from the caste of Slaves to Professionals. Communication with producers, understanding of the production process, connections with businessmen created the basis for forming the Entrepreneur role. In the Entrepreneur role, he began intensive contact with financiers, which pushed him to master the role of the same name himself. This gave another impulse for business development and expanding the circle of acquaintances with significant people, including politicians. Such communication made it possible to master the Politician role and finally secured the Successful Creator in the caste of Businessmen.
The path of the Successful Creator: (1–12SM)>(2–12T)>(3–9T)>(4–9E)>(5–8E)>(6–8F)>(7–8P)>(8–3P).
Talented Loser
The Talented Loser is my favorite character because of how common he is. It feels like everyone with above-average intellectual abilities falls into this category. As the saying goes, "Woe from Wit!" To be honest, [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC) is written first and foremost for exactly such people.
Having entered university, the future Talented Loser had no particular problems with studying. Everything came to him effortlessly. He amazed his peers with speed and originality of thinking, was always creative, vivid, showed great promise. They tried to persuade him to stay at the university and go into science. But he dragged his feet about agreeing: science did not bring the income he wanted and, besides, implied too long and difficult a path to success. Our hero always found it very hard to find himself a mentor--he was too smart for most of them. He treated any advice skeptically, since he usually knew himself what the best move was. And in general, he did not feel a need for someone else's intellectual patronage--his own ideas were enough.
His buddy--a small-time merchant--told him about the difficulties of his small business. The Talented Loser immediately proposed a new scheme, which they implemented together. The business promised good profits, so our hero finally cut all ties with science. But after some time, problems piled up that accompany any trade business. It became uninteresting, and he was pulled back into science. He went to work at the university, got a grant; things were going pretty well, but there still was not enough money. Friends invited him into their company to do PR. The Talented Loser talked with entrepreneurs and proposed to them schemes for optimizing their businesses. One of them offered him a stake in a new business. Our hero agreed and in a short time successfully developed the venture to a serious level.
He carried everything on his back, which led to problems. In the end he burned out from the strain, interest in the topic faded, and he was pulled into creative work. He created a creative business with the money he had earned. But the pull toward new topics was stronger than the drive to make money. The funds ran out, and the business was turned into a division of a holding whose leadership our hero strongly impressed with his fiery speeches. The owner of the holding believed his idea and gave him authority and resources, but during the test period far less money was earned than was spent. On top of that, conflict began to grow with the owner and his entourage. As a result, our hero left the business, selling his stake for pennies.
He started from scratch again and decided to go work as an employee. Thanks to his abilities he quickly found a good position, got acquainted with a shareholder, and after some time became the top executive of the company. The situation repeated itself, but on a larger scale. Conflicts, inefficiency, a break with the employer. The company was liquidated, and the Talented Loser was again without work. He did not sit idle for long. He was invited into a project by partners of the former shareholder, but the cooperation did not last. The Talented Loser was once again let down by a taste for conflict and a desire to prove he was right. After some time he was offered the post of head of a major media holding, but once there he failed to build relationships with the owner's entourage and once again left the employer's company.
He met an interesting guy while diving. They talked. The guy outlined the project theme, and our hero instantly proposed an unconventional approach to implementing it. Impressive prospects were emerging. However, the new friend died tragically, and the remaining partners preferred not to get involved with the Talented Loser--too smart and too slick. He went into consulting, but felt no particular joy in it. The need to perform all sorts of rituals was very stressful--a mandatory condition of long-term relationships with serious clients. The projects did not inspire him. He was bored. He earned and immediately spent. He slid through life like an autumn leaf on water, nowhere feeling in his place. In the end he got tired of searching and took a job--as a manager in a company. He worked there until retirement, and when he went on his deserved rest, he began teaching and rented out one room in his apartment to have something to live on.
From the point of view of [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC): talent trained our hero to get everything without effort, and because of that the need to work and focus on anything bored him. He early became an excellent Specialist and Teacher. Money and fame were never values for him--he earned as needed. The Talented Loser did not master the Dealmaker and Manager roles, but played the Entrepreneur and Financier roles well, since he was an intelligent person. And also the Leader role--he did not lack personal charisma. But with the Politician role it never worked out: the ability to negotiate, find compromise, adapt did not become part of his skills.
As a result, he quickly left the caste of Slaves and got stuck in the caste of Entrepreneurs, where he was not capable of creating a large business. The Specialist role did not provide the necessary level of pragmatism, and the absence of the Manager role prevented him from becoming a good leader. In addition, his inner Specialist constantly competed with employees and tried to teach them instead of using them. His Leader produced a positive reaction both in partners and in subordinates. But the Talented Loser could not monetize his charisma due to the lack, in his arsenal, of the Dealmaker and Manager roles. As a result, he constantly risked sliding back into the caste of Slaves as soon as money ran out. At the sunset of his career, he had to return to the Specialist and Teacher roles, since life circumstances made all other roles irrelevant.
The path of the Talented Loser: (1–12S)>(2–12TH)>(3–12E)>(4–8E)>(5–8F)>(6–8L)>(7–12L)>(8–12E).
Wretched Workhorse
The Wretched Workhorse was a good guy. Loyal and dim. He willingly carried out orders, got along with management, and had no special ambitions. For him there was always only one role--Specialist. He worked his whole life in one place. He saved up neither for a car nor for a second apartment. For vacation he went to the village. He managed to instill in his children a love of labor. They graduated from state universities. They never left the family nest. Closer to retirement, the Wretched Workhorse got seriously ill. He could not get good treatment--there was no money. Soon he died. The children mourned a bit, but secretly were glad for such an outcome, since living space was freed up.
From the point of view of [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC): all his life the Wretched Workhorse was a Specialist. Not a bad one. Once he was appointed to manage people because he turned out to be the oldest and most experienced in the team. Alas, our hero blew it, since he did not understand what it means to lead, and kept redoing everyone's work for them. They demoted him, transferring him back into Specialist work. In that role he went into retirement.
The path of the Wretched Workhorse: (1–12S)>(2–12M)>(3–12S).
