PART 5. FATE AND THE MEANING OF LIFE

Will, Heredity, Karma
Most people living on this planet naively believe they have complete freedom to choose their life path. Like in Andrey Makarevich's song: "the road is open to us for many, many years..." Like any emotional-poetic slogan, the statement has a right to exist. However, if you recall the continuation of the song by Mashina Vremeni, another line comes to mind: "the train will pass only where the track is laid." Something to think about.
In other words, our life is the expression of the sum of our desires, adjusted by the baggage of heredity and the environment we came out of. Often this baggage is called "personal background," but I propose, very conditionally, so to speak, metaphorically, to call it within our book karma.
Let's sort out what it includes. We are born with a certain set of qualities. They come to us first of all from heredity, which determines our mental and physical abilities, predisposition to disease, overall energy level, and much more. We are born into a specific family, which at first shapes our value system, sets life reference points, provides financial standing, raises us, in the end. That family lives in a very specific city and country, which naturally determines our mentality. Everything listed makes up karma. That load of qualities, attitudes, and circumstances that is issued to us by the mere fact of our existence in this world.
But besides that, we are beings endowed with free will. This gives us the ability to set goals and strive for what our preferences and values dictate. That is, to make conscious effort in order to move forward. And here, when the direction is found and the goal is set, it turns out that heredity, upbringing, and our starting environment did not give us something. We lack resources, knowledge, skills, personal qualities. Or, on the contrary, we got a lot of excess at the start--hangups, complexes, superstitions, prejudices, laziness, fatalism, and so on.
If we manage to track the presence in our karma of all this ballast, then it is our direct duty to get rid of it. We acquire new knowledge and skills. Our knowledge is what gives us breadth of perspective, and skills are what allow us to take useful action. To do that, we develop the necessary character traits. Or we consciously get rid of the ones that get in the way. That is, we have the ability to deliberately change our personality. Moreover, we can rethink our value system, set new goals, fill circumstances with new meaning, cultivate ambition within ourselves.
In truth, there are no limits to the scale of our aspirations. There are no boundaries. The insurmountability of any obstacles is subjective. That is, in other words, on one pan of our life scales lies karma--the given of our starting conditions in the Game. On the other, our will. And the stronger the will, the less karma matters. Historical examples--biographies of extraordinary people who achieved success in spite of their origin, education level, or physical data--illustrate this thesis perfectly.
Will--mighty and unbreakable--is what determines the human path. Of course, if you have it. The question arises: where does will come from? Let's be honest: most people around us are deprived of it. They drift slowly to where their karma persistently pushes them. They do not resist, do not try to change direction. So thanks to what is a powerful force born in a human creature, one that makes them act in defiance of heredity and environment? What mental processes force us to focus all our efforts on achieving one goal? On stubborn movement upward?
The Source of Willpower
Will appears when there is meaning in life. And we gain meaning in life when a conscious aspiration appears, a clear direction of movement, and an understanding of what it is for that we need to overcome difficulties and change ourselves. That is why I oppose karma to will: karma as the sum of the past, and will as an orientation toward the future.
So, our fate is formed under the influence of karma, will, and desires. Then what is will? Will is the inner force that allows us to overcome obstacles and persistently move in the direction of obtaining what we want. It is trained like any other human ability. And at the same time, will can degrade if it is not developed.
Desire is harder. In essence, it is desire that determines the point of application of our will. If the desire is superficial and small, then even having strong will and obtaining what you sought, you will get something indistinct. If you have a worthy desire, then even with bad karma and weak will you will be able to achieve grand results. It's just that for this you will first have to level up your willpower, and then, with its help, overcome all obstacles.
The final destination depends on the scale and depth of your desire, on the strength of your intention, in the end. Karma and other external factors are only obstacles. And with any obstacles, with will and patience, you can cope. However, if you are unable to determine the right direction of movement, everything is useless. Desire, motivation, meaning in life--this is the inner fuel that powers your development, supplies it with energy.
Many people are afraid to desire something big, because they do not know where to get the resources. You should not be afraid. Resources always appear when strong desire and powerful motivation arise. Not the other way around. The greatest mistake is to sit and wait until you have enough energy, connections, money to finally start acting. Spoiler: you will never have enough. And that is good news. Because, among other things, the lack of necessary resources creates emotional tension, which in turn helps you achieve your goals.
As I said earlier, the cause of any grand changes in this world is a person. It is they who transform reality and fill it with meaning. Without their conscious will, nothing happens with the world--it simply goes feral. Plants, minerals, the elements do not have free will. They do not realize designs; they merely exist. Only a human being can consciously want something--and then their life is filled with something besides the primitive struggle for shelter and food. Namely, effort toward achieving a certain higher goal.
Desires, Meaning, and Values
People constantly face problems connected with their foundational desires and the meaning of life. These problems follow from the fact that many do not know:
- what they want;
- what one can want;
- what has meaning for them.
For most of us, conversations about the meaning of life fall into a zone of elevated emotional tension. Start asking a randomly chosen person what they live for, and you will get either a set of stereotypical phrases or a reaction of frustration: "Oh God, let's not talk about such complicated things!" And all because in our society the topic of the meaning of life is overly dramatized. On the one hand, it is treated as something sacred, and on the other, as mandatory for reflection and the search for answers. Apparently we owe the latter to literature classes devoted to the inner turmoil of Chatsky and Pechorin.
I know what I'm talking about. In conversations with my clients and subscribers, I have repeatedly had to ask the question, "What is the meaning of your life?"--and watch confusion on people's faces. As if they were schoolkids whom a strict teacher caught unprepared. I had to soften: "Okay, don't strain--just tell me what you want." That question was easier for my interlocutors, but their answers were completely unstructured. Nevertheless, all answers can be reduced to several categories:
- to possess certain things or objects;
- to be a wealthy person;
- to do an activity that brings satisfaction and recognition;
- to have social status, to gain fame;
- to create a work of art, make a scientific discovery, invent something breakthrough;
- to study or master something;
- to live in a certain place or have the ability to travel;
- to make someone happy: save them from poverty or illness, give them a future;
- to be with someone important, close, loved;
- for the people close to them--parents, friends, sisters, brothers--to be okay.
In short, nothing new. But the search even for these obvious answers, in my observation, required significant effort from people. At the same time, it was much easier for them to say what they did not want:
- poverty;
- illness for themselves and their loved ones;
- restrictions of freedom;
- uninteresting work that brings no satisfaction;
- to live in uncomfortable conditions;
- war and all kinds of cataclysms and disasters.
Based on the first and second groups of answers, you can compile a list of people's values and anti-values. That is, the things and phenomena we strive for in the scale of life and the things we desperately fear.
VALUES:
- a decent lifestyle and pleasant leisure;
- a comfortable space for living: a home, city, neighborhood;
- a suitable circle of communication;
- property;
- reputation and decent relationships with other people;
- high self-esteem, self-respect;
- health, overall well-being;
- freedom to take actions aimed at achieving all the previous points.
ANTI-VALUES:
- illness;
- poverty and losses;
- bad reputation, ruined relationships with people;
- restriction of freedom.
An understanding of values and anti-values is necessary for us to sort out once and for all what the meaning of life is. This is the first step on the path to finding it.
Life Principles and Inner Harmony
If, in the context of the meaning of life, you raise the question of values and anti-values, you cannot forget principles. These are reasonable limitations that keep you from harming yourself and others in the process of obtaining values. Principles determine which methods are ethical and which are not. This, in turn, provides a person with inner harmony. Any neglect of principles leads to negative consequences in the long run, and therefore to a likely loss of the values obtained. In other words, if you, for example, decide to commit robbery, there is a high probability that later you will end up in prison or become a victim of a robbery even bolder than your own.
For some people--and they are the majority--one of the main principles is not to harm those around you. As the saying goes, do not do to others what you do not want done to you. This is a moral limitation that forbids using certain methods that seem effective, because they harm other people. A person with moral constraints would rather accept being unable to obtain what they want than harm someone. The negative experience of guilt is stronger than the negative emotions connected, for example, with poverty.
Thus a person constantly finds themselves faced with a choice: which suffering to refuse. If the fear of losing personal values is stronger, they cross moral boundaries; if they are more afraid of guilt and of losing their reputation, they do not. The presence of moral constraints is fundamental. We consciously move toward it so that in the long run we do not lose inner harmony.
Some neglect these constraints and obtain values by dishonorable means, but over time, as a rule, they repent. Happiness obtained at someone else's expense sooner or later gathers around a person those who create problems for them and nullify all their efforts. Thus, ethical life principles allow you to achieve goals in the most harmonious ways. In ways that will not force you to suffer pangs of conscience.
This is important because, according to Maslow's theory, when a person satisfies their lower needs, their desires shift into the area of higher needs connected with social realization and recognition of our authority by other people. That is, the moment you acquire money and property, their significance falls, and questions of reputation, public recognition, respect from other people come to the forefront. And here any unethical methods come back to you threefold, like a boomerang. Your results will be acknowledged if they did not turn into other people's misfortunes and losses. Otherwise people will begin to despise and hate you, which in turn at any moment can turn into unpleasant incidents. And that is already a risk of losing everything you worked so hard for. And the issue here is not the triumph of some abstract universal justice. It's simpler: someone's patience eventually breaks.
What Success Is and How It Relates to the Meaning of Life
All this simple reasoning allows us to draw a conclusion: if a person has obtained the values they need, has not burdened their life with anti-values, has not crossed moral boundaries, and now feels inner harmony--they are successful.
Among other things, the person has gained:
- the desired lifestyle and leisure;
- housing they dreamed of;
- a pleasant circle of communication;
- luxury items;
- a positive reputation;
- gratitude from loved ones;
- high self-esteem;
- the ability to maintain their health.
Notice: I am not saying what exactly a person should be doing. We are talking only about results that can be felt and touched.
Success is a certain level of self-esteem, a sense of satisfaction with life. That is, the key point is a person's subjective evaluation of their achievements. Therefore, the feeling of success is not connected with the actual volume of values obtained. Everything depends on the level of claims: someone considers themselves successful living on an average salary in a small apartment, while someone is sure they are a loser while living in a mansion and having a solid bank account. When a poor man with 1,000 rubles in his pocket loses 900, it's unpleasant, but not fatal. When a banker with a fortune of $100 million loses $90 million, he commits suicide.
Of course, it is important that your income provide the necessary standard of living and give you the possibility of self-realization. That is an axiom. But everything beyond that is already a passion that devours you from within. However, if this passion leads to the fact that a person:
- has obtained what they sought;
- has avoided anti-values;
- has not crossed moral boundaries;
- feels inner harmony, then they are successful.
In this case, passion is nothing more than a powerful motivator that pushes them forward when others retreat. People build fortunes not because they don't have enough money, but because they have tasted the management of their own fate, felt the thrill of creating their own world.
And here we smoothly move on to the connection between the meaning of life and success. Imagine you are offered a job that you don't like, promises no career growth, and brings in miserable money. Or, on the contrary, you are offered interesting work with good income and the possibility of further development. The choice is obvious. The first job has no meaning for you; the second is filled with it to the brim.
The meaning of life is not an idea, but emotions you get as a result of evaluating the prospects connected with your activity. If a startup, a creative project, or your everyday work leads you to obtaining the desired values, then the activity--whatever it is called--has meaning. If it doesn't, it doesn't. Put differently: if prospects seem favorable to you, your life fills with meaning, and you stop painfully searching for an answer to the question, "Why do I live?"
I am often asked: show me the thing that will fill my existence with meaning. And I answer: it can be anything. From cutting and sewing to working at a machine-building plant. From info-business to raising cattle. No one can say with one-hundred-percent certainty whether their chosen path will lead them to success or not. Which means meaning is provided by the occupation whose effectiveness you believe in. The key word is believe. The meaning of life is faith that your work, lifestyle, and chain of fateful choices will bring you to the point of "it all worked out the way I dreamed."
As long as you believe, your life is filled with meaning. That gives you energy to move toward your goals. As soon as faith disappears, motivation disappears as well. Movement stops.
Let's sum it up. Any activity can lead you to success if you believe in it. Does that mean faith is enough? The answer is no. Faith is not enough; you also need knowledge and skills. But without faith, any knowledge and skills are useless. With them you can create, at best, meaningless bustle. Only faith can turn this bustle into purposeful movement. And that is where the difficulties begin.
