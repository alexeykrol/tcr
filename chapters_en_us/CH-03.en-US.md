## PART 2. SOCIETY AND ITS CASTES

### The Key Information About Our World
In this world there are only people, because only they are capable of changing reality. There is nothing here besides people. Everything else is a heap of living and dead matter that has no meaning without people. A human being is mortal, weak, has no power to change reality directly, has no connection to the Creator. Therefore a person always hungers for more, fears loss, unites with others like themselves, and searches for meaning.

Together, people are capable of transforming reality. They unite and act when there is an idea that gives meaning and direction to their efforts. A sense of meaning arises whenever a person believes that actions will lead them to what they desire. Without an idea and without meaning, a person cannot develop; they can only survive, maintaining a minimally sufficient level of well-being. To achieve anything, you must interact with ideas and people.

### Value Systems of the Inhabitants of Planet Earth
The main problem in our world is limited access to resources. Everything is built around a kind of global feeding trough, and there is a constant overt or invisible struggle for access to it. The meaning of existence for the overwhelming majority of the inhabitants of our world boils down to getting a good spot near the trough--a place where it is warm, bright, and safe.

Resource scarcity has formed the dominant value system of the inhabitants of planet Earth. It looks like this:
- People survive by uniting and jointly using various resources.
- Resource scarcity is a mandatory condition for the existence of humanity.
- The most valuable resources are non-renewable. In early stages these are underground minerals; in later stages they are places suitable for living, clean air, water, and food.
- A human being is a renewable resource, therefore their life has no value. Millions are killed for oil, but no one has ever stopped consuming oil for the sake of a human being.
- People, by and large, are tools for carrying small loads into hard-to-reach places, destroying valuables, and extracting and creating new valuables. In rare cases, a person is a tool for solving problems and generating ideas. Therefore their access to resources must be limited and they must be paid the bare minimum so they can carry loads, create, destroy, solve problems, and generate ideas for those who own resources.
- A person who does not own resources owns nothing--not even the thoughts in their head. They are already in debt by the mere fact of being born, because they consume and therefore depend on those who have resources.
- The one who rules is the one who has a monopoly right to resources, as well as the power to keep them.
- The battle for resources is regulated by special rules. That is why private property is declared sacred, and encroachment on someone else's property is one of the gravest crimes.
- For those who have gained greater control over property and resources, it is important to have rules and forces for defense, so legal systems, armies, and so on are created.
- All ongoing changes are aimed at protecting property.

A person on Earth, by themselves, is a weak creature with minimal capabilities, therefore the only power people have is in unity. Truly large resources are simply the union of a large number of people with their resources. When what unites them disappears, these enormous resources disappear as well. For example, as happened with the Soviet Union.

That is why on planet Earth there is not only a pragmatic but also an altruistic system of values--values mixed with friendship, love, patriotism, loyalty, everything that creates strong bonds among members of society. Otherwise, people would not be inclined toward cooperation based not only on benefit, but also on a striving for good. Throughout life, a person balances between altruistic and pragmatic value systems. We constantly observe the conflict between cooperation and competition, from time to time finding ourselves at its epicenter.

### The Caste System of Human Civilization
Resource scarcity, the dominance of a pragmatic system of values, and the need for unity have led to the fact that human society resembles a huge pyramid. Each of its levels is a caste. The more influence and scarce resources are concentrated in the hands of representatives of a given caste, the higher it is. It is almost impossible to move into higher castes while skipping the intermediate ones. The road upward runs through castes, and each caste is like a sovereign state. It has its own language, its own laws, its own identifying marks, and also its own scope of rights to property and resources.

Let's be honest: representatives of the lower castes have virtually no real rights. They are forced to constantly work for the higher castes. As an ancient Roman saying goes: "For our own--everything; for enemies--the law."

A person feels that their life is filled with meaning only while they are moving from lower castes to higher ones. Not out of a thirst for power or the satisfaction of ambitions--those are motivational phantoms. It is simpler. They feel that only in the higher castes will they gain enough freedom to realize their plans, to feel satisfaction with life. The higher the caste, the more power and strength it gives for controlling one's fate.

Sometimes readers ask me: what if I don't need power? Good question. Most people do not understand what true power is. They think it is the ability to control the fates of millions of people. But in reality, true power implies control over the fate of one person--yourself. Until you become the master of your fate, you are doomed to live in a world subordinated to someone else's rules and therefore to always be in someone else's power. You will follow someone else's goals. You will act in accordance with someone else's desires--desires imposed by clever propaganda, marketing, traditions, and false prejudices. You may not realize it, but society actively tells you which desires are correct, acceptable, worthy of approval, and which are not. Therefore, if only in order to want something consciously, you need to take the blinders off.

Each caste is like a country. And just as every sovereign country has borders, castes have inter-caste barriers that complicate the penetration of representatives of other castes.

### Opportunities and Needs
A person's belonging to one caste or another unambiguously determines their social status and financial position in society. Therefore the first important conclusion is: if your idea of success is connected with achieving a desired social-professional and financial status, then you will inevitably end up in the corresponding caste. Each specific caste provides specific opportunities, which is inseparably connected with the physical and creative aspects of your life. This does not mean that money is the main thing, but many goals are easier to achieve when you have a higher social and financial position.

At the same time, making a higher position an end in itself is a typical mistake for most people. The striving for status and money yields neither. A caste is not the goal of your path, but its consequence. Belonging to a certain caste sets not so much your financial level as the level of opportunities for self-realization and bringing your plans to life. The higher the caste, the more grand those plans can be.

Resources and limitations both play a big role. Belonging to a lower caste means you live in a not-so-good neighborhood, in an apartment with a minimal set of amenities, you eat poorly, and you experience permanent stress at work. This increases the likelihood of disease, while your ability to obtain quality medical care is limited. It becomes a vicious circle. Limitations and the absence of opportunities provoke health problems that you are also unable to solve adequately. And conversely, in the higher castes you can achieve any goals and deal with almost any unpleasantness.

I propose a simple experiment. Write down on a piece of paper all your needs regarding housing, health, education, leisure, and so on. Let these be needs for the coming year--there is no point in trying to embrace the immensity. Now, next to each need, put the amount of money that satisfying it will require. Add to the list the costs of regularly meeting basic needs: food, commuting to and from work, utilities, and so on. Now total everything up and compare your current annual income with what you want. How large is the gap?

If we are talking about a one-and-a-half, two, three times increase, then it is obvious: you need to move up the caste pyramid. You can work yourself to the bone, beat out a raise, but all these efforts will not solve the problem. Only a change of caste will ensure a fundamental change in your level of income and consumption. There are no other options. The obvious conclusion: if you want power over your fate, a normal quality of life, and creative self-realization, then at minimum you need to get out of the slave castes in which most people reside.

Why does only moving up the caste pyramid ensure a fundamental change in the level of well-being? Because different castes have different natures of income. We will talk about that next.

### The Nature of Income in Different Castes
We divide castes into lower, middle, and upper classes. Each class has its own nature of income. Let's figure out what that means.

**The Nature of Income in the Lower Castes**
Representatives of the lower castes sell their time and their ability to solve professional tasks. They work for an employer and receive a salary that the employer sets. As a rule, it is enough exactly to provide the standard of living typical for the slave caste--no more and no less. You could say they sell labor on a market ruled by the buyer, that is, by the one who hires them. The buyer dictates the terms, leaving no chance for freedom of choice. Representatives of the slave castes depend on having a job and a steady cash flow. If incoming cash flow stops, their situation immediately becomes dire. Slaves have insignificant income, so it is difficult for them to build reserves and therefore to be independent.

**The Nature of Income in the Middle Castes**
Representatives of the middle castes sell not only their time and their ability to solve professional tasks, but also their reputation. Reputation creates additional value that makes it possible to dictate the price of work to the employer. Representatives of the middle castes offer services in a seller's market, which means they can choose employers, projects, and job responsibilities. Higher income makes it possible to maintain a decent quality of life, delegate some personal tasks to assistants, and accumulate financial reserves.

Thus, the level of independence of representatives of the middle castes is significantly higher. Nevertheless, there is one important drawback: their income depends on daily work. Stop working, and the tap closes; the flow of money dries up. This is a serious limitation. You cannot receive more money than you are able to earn through your direct labor. No matter how expensive a specialist you are, there are only 24 hours in a day. Even so, the most successful representatives of these castes receive fabulous fees that allow them to simultaneously be in the caste of Rentiers.

**The Nature of Income in the Upper Castes**
What distinguishes representatives of the upper castes from everyone else? They not only work for themselves, but also give work to others. People and money work for them. Hence a qualitative leap in possibilities. Your income is not limited if you can hire an unlimited number of people. The only constraining factor is demand for your product or services. At the same time, representatives of the upper castes have passive income that capitalizes itself. Current profit so far exceeds current expenses that you can not only build a reserve, but also invest it to multiply it. With a sufficient amount of money, income from investing turns out to be even higher than income from the main activity.

As a result, representatives of the upper castes own a system for obtaining and reproducing money. The availability of financial means no longer depends on personal effort. A representative of the upper caste can do anything, but they will still have a growing cash flow and a gigantic field of opportunities.
