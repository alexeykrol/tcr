PART 4. HOW TO GROW IN THIS WORLD

A General Growth Strategy Based on Changing Roles
In youth, a person is not very educated, has no experience, no professional skills, no understanding of how the world works. Therefore, only physical labor is available to them, and so they belong to the caste of Slaves. But if they study actively, they gradually move into Specialists. Their horizons expand. A person begins to see new levels of being, to realize life goals and tasks, can engage in self-improvement, and thus--grow socially.
On the one hand, having reached a certain level of professional development, you can share your knowledge--go teach. On the other, by teaching, you yourself reach true mastery. And at that point it is time to learn how to sell yourself. That is, we are talking about consistently mastering roles: first Specialist, then Teacher, and finally--Dealmaker. The last role is the key one. It implies a role fork, because it gives you the resource to move into the caste of Professionals. Once there, a person has already learned to trade their competencies, but they still lack the knowledge and skills to make a fortune. The Professional spends everything they earn. They do not know how to accumulate and create self-sustaining business systems.
You can get into the higher caste of Businessmen only by mastering the roles of Entrepreneur, Manager, and Financier. If the Professional manages this and moves into the caste of Businessman (or, alternatively, Top Manager), that is where their organic growth stops. Further development goes along a financial-investment path. Having created an industry empire, becoming simultaneously a Top Manager and a Businessman, a person understands that they are tied to power and society, and therefore begins to pave a path into politics. By mastering the Politician role, the Businessman has a chance to move into the caste of Oligarchs, because the highest advantages in business can be obtained only thanks to relationships with power and society. This is not a consequence of Russian specificity; it is simply that the larger the business, the stronger the mutual influence between it and society as a whole.

Now that we have an understanding of the real structure of society, we can match castes and roles and get a matrix. Vertically it will list castes, and horizontally--roles. This matrix can serve as a map on which we will demonstrate all possible strategies for achieving success in this world. It shows what and why you need to do, what results it leads to, and what obstacles it allows you to overcome along the way.

Each caste includes far from all roles. It is obvious that in the lower castes there are only those roles that have minimal growth potential, and in the upper castes--only high. For example, the caste of Slaves does not imply the roles of Advisor, Entrepreneur, Financier, Politician, and Leader. Likewise, these roles do not exist in the castes of Gendarmes and Professionals. In the lower castes, people play only the roles of Doer, Specialist, Teacher, Dealmaker, Manager. These same roles can be encountered in the castes of Professionals and Entrepreneurs. People of the upper castes perform exclusively higher roles. Having the appropriate role in your repertoire is your pass into the upper castes, and also a guarantee that you will secure your place there.
The Dominant Role Determines Your True Choice
A person perceives the world and acts through the prism of their dominant role. That is, they see what is happening around them, respond to events and people, show or do not show initiative in the way their role requires. For example, the Specialist thinks in categories of problem-solving. They are focused on methods, technologies, approaches that help them achieve professional goals. Because of this, their mind runs a continuous process of searching for optimal solutions: what headline to come up with for a magazine article, how to organize delivery of goods, what prescription to write for a patient, and so on.
It is enough to transform your key role, and your way of thinking and acting changes immediately, and then your whole life. But for this, you first need to determine which role dominates in your case. And only after that begin consciously changing it. I will say right away: this is a very difficult task, because the dominant role is exceptionally stable. Often it is so tightly intertwined with your basic values and attitudes that changing it is possible only through the managed destruction of personality. Usually, this path implies that you consciously put yourself into situations that force you to make decisions that are atypical for you. True, for this path to be productive, you need to get feedback from someone experienced: what the correct decision would be for the target role and the corresponding caste.
[Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC) says: first you need to change your role and skills, and only then, finding yourself in one situation or another, you will begin to make the right choice at a subconscious level and stop wasting time on what does not lead you where you need to go.
Psychologists claim: solving a problem begins with recognizing and accepting it. That is all true, but only partly. Ninety-nine percent of people on this planet realize that it is better to be rich and healthy than poor and sick. We all know that. And we understand that we are not doing what we want, but what we are forced to do. We realize the need for changes and begin working on it: attend seminars, read literature on psychology and self-development, listen to podcasts. But we do not get results. Fundamentally, nothing changes.
Why? Because our vision, behavior, choice of situations and preferences, people, books, leisure--however you slice it--is determined by our dominant role. In essence, we subconsciously want what this role wants. That is how we were raised. We were given these values and taught to derive pleasure from them. A role is one of the basic parts of personality. Not something external in relation to you, but you yourself in certain situations. That is precisely why you do not notice that you are playing a specific role--because, broadly speaking, it is not separable from your personality. And even if you decide to change, you subconsciously begin to sabotage this process. Because change means not only creating a new personality, but also destroying the old one. That is, if you want to become a different person, "kill" your old self. And that hurts. Very much.
Transformation Is Destruction, and Destruction Is the Key to Change
True learning is the destruction of the old personality and the creation of a new one. And here we need an objective assessment from the outside. And it is absolutely not necessary for it to come from another person. It is important one way or another to be able to track the degree of your transformation--to see milestones by which you can check: have I reached the necessary amount of personal change or not. Evaluating yourself is a thankless exercise. And here the caste metaphor comes to our aid. Our observations of ourselves and our changes can be subjective, superficial, tendentious, deceptive. But if our personality changes, then its perception changes, and most importantly--interaction with the external world, which immediately produces results. That result is a change in your financial and social position. That result is a consequence of our actions. But that result is outside you, and therefore you can use it to evaluate how much you have truly changed. And by the stability of results--understand how stable the transformation is and how your dominant role has been reshaped.
Most people are frightened by the need to transform their personality. At the same time, we forget that we not only lose our old self, but also gain a new one. Understand once and for all: your former roles are pathetic and chaotic. You are going to have to build a new harmonious personality, more productive in its desires, goals, and skills. Fundamental spiritual values are not lost, but replaced or supplemented with new ones. You become more perfect. A balanced approach is necessary not only to raise your financial and spiritual level, but also to remain, and sometimes become, a human being.
One important conclusion that can be drawn from [Theory of Castes and Roles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PDHZRCC): in order to build a correct strategy for achieving social, financial, and personal goals, you need to systematically change your roles. The ones that at a certain moment begin to block your movement up the hierarchy. Roles impose stereotypical thinking and actions on us. Yes, they can be useful, but only until they begin to limit us. Especially when you need to make a leap to the next level.
There is a common myth that in front of every person lies an infinite number of life roads. It is an illusion. An infinite number of roads lies only before the one who is the master of their fate. For the one who does not belong to themselves, there is only one path--the path of a slave. Nevertheless, every person has the opportunity to approach the border of the caste of Slaves in order to make a jump and overcome an inter-caste barrier. This feat has several strategies. We will consider them carefully, because different strategies may suit different people. As the saying goes, find yours.
Any strategy has two dimensions. First, the external one--your everyday life. Second, the internal one--the transformation of your dominant and accompanying roles. The most important is the internal dimension of the strategy, because it is aimed at changing your personality. Until you change yourself, your perception, your role attitudes, you will not be able to see the world differently, recognize new opportunities, and therefore you will not behave in a new way.
And vice versa, as soon as your roles transform, you will begin to see and attract new opportunities that existed before as well, but remained inaccessible to you, because your eyes were closed. As soon as they open, you can not only see new prospects, but also act toward them differently, with a different result. Here the inside-out principle works: change yourself--and the world will change, and after it--your existence in it.
Now that we have figured out what castes and roles are, it is time to move on to the concept of "human fate." We will figure out what a happy fate is and what an unhappy one is, and most importantly: what exactly happiness and success depend on across the whole of life.
