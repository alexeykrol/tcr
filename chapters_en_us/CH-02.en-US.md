# CH-02 -- PART 1. HUMAN LIFE AS A GAME

## PART 1. HUMAN LIFE AS A GAME

### The World Has Grown More Complex
Over the past fifty years, the world has become incredibly complex. Simplified meanings of life have faded away, non-obvious principles of social advancement have started to operate, and society has split into countless groups at war with one another. Knowledge becomes obsolete faster than we can acquire it; the number of rules and regulations grows; social barriers and various forms of segregation arise. Niches for the realization of free initiative are shrinking not only in business, but also in science and art.

It is increasingly difficult to find someone who can tell you where to go, where your place is, what you want, what you should want, how to live, where to live, what to consume. What is the meaning of life anyway?

The overwhelming majority of people on this planet not only do not have the right goals, but do not even really know what they want; they do not understand the world around them or the laws that operate in it; they do not have planning skills; and, in general, they have a very narrow horizon. It is becoming harder and harder to extract the information you need. As the world changes, the rules of life wisdom are changing as well. The effectiveness of familiar growth strategies declines. According to data cited in various sources, no more than 10-12 percent of all adherents of personal growth actually achieve radical changes in their lives. And by the statements of motivational trainers themselves, that figure is no more than one percent.

At minimum, there are three reasons for this state of affairs:
- not understanding how the world we live in is structured;
- an unfriendly environment and conflicts of interest;
- the problem of goal-setting.

### A False Understanding of the World We Live In
The meaning of life follows from a system of values--that is, a list of what is important and useful to have, what is declared valuable. A system of values, in turn, rests on a worldview. A worldview is our ideas about what rules and laws operate in this world, our belief in what is possible and what is not. Values and worldview do not reflect reality; they only provide notions about it. Those notions rely less on facts and more on faith and convictions.

All these convictions, meanings, values, and worldview are formed spontaneously by parents and the surrounding environment. That is, convictions can be created, transformed, and erased. It is your worldview and your values that determine who you are, what you are capable of achieving in this world, what is possible for you and what is not. Everyone builds their own universe. Everyone lives in the world they themselves generate. This is a correct thesis; it forces you to act.

### Imposed Meanings, Worldview, and Values
A person's fate depends on them, but only if they have freedom of choice. To have freedom of choice, you must have freedom of thought and feeling. Even slaves in a slaveholding society were able to change their fates and the fate of the world. But today, when a person is under continuous pressure from advertising and political propaganda, they are unable to manage their emotions. They are unable to manage their thoughts, either, because those thoughts are managed by mass media, biased bloggers, and fleeting trends.

And it would be wrong to assume that, in such a state, a person bears responsibility for their choice, or is even capable of making one. This state is called artificial ignorance. Ignorance manifests in the fact that the overwhelming majority of people on this planet do not know what goals and meanings in life even exist; they cannot distinguish their own goal from pseudo-useful desires imposed by aggressive marketing.

If a person has no goals, no idea of what they can want and where it is located, all other conversations are meaningless. As the caterpillar from Lewis Carroll's psychedelic book *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* said: "If you don't know where you're going, does it matter which road you take?" I'll add to that: does it matter whether you walk or stand still?

Our aspirations are limited by our meager experience, as well as by the experience of our parents and friends--people who belong to the same circle as we do. Their life experience is not much richer, and sometimes even poorer, than yours. In most cases, this applies to our parents as well. You can rely on books, but, alas, the books you read and the films you watch give you absolutely nothing. As a result, you cannot chart the correct course of your life because you do not have a map. What you are using is not a map, but either advertising brochures or products whose goal is not your advancement at all, but pumping money out of your wallet.

It becomes a vicious circle: you cannot realize yourself because you do not move with purpose; you cannot formulate concrete goals; you do not know how the world really works. But that's not all.

### The Problem of an Unfriendly Environment and Conflicts of Interest
The personal growth industry insists that the causes of all evils lie only in your laziness, insecurity, and other internal problems. The key claim: everything depends on you. I am not saying that laziness, insecurity, and ignorance are not cunning enemies standing between you and success. They are indeed serious obstacles, and we will talk about them later.

But not everything depends on you. Some things depend on the rules and laws of the surrounding world, as well as the people living in it. Keep in mind that besides us, there are other beings in this world who also build their own universe. Moreover, much of this world has already been built by someone before us. And those who came before us, when they built this world and its rules, did not think about us. At that time they already had their own children, friends, acquaintances, their own property, and their own aspirations. Perhaps this thesis is not politically correct, but it forces you to act correctly. Correctly--meaning, taking into account not only your own desires, but also the rules and desires of those who arrived here before you.

If this concept bothers you, compare a popular psychology training to the preparation of special agents for carrying out an illegal mission in a hostile environment. Special agents are not told that everything is inside them and that they need to know and accept themselves. They are prepared for concrete situations and the likely behavior of an adversary; they are taught to act as effectively as possible under the assumed circumstances. That is why special agents are effective: their actions are pragmatic and focused on the mission. It is not enough to only change yourself, to endlessly stare at your own belly button. It is important to know the laws by which the surrounding world is built and to be guided by them. At least in order to stop being an obedient puppet in the hands of those who pursue interests far from your own.

### The Problem of Conscious Goal-Setting
Even if you master a set of magical techniques for making friends and influencing people, start following principles of high effectiveness, and learn to love yourself, it will not help. Imagine a ship moving across the open sea. In order to arrive at its destination port, the crew measures the ship's position relative to geographic landmarks and its speed. This makes it possible to calculate the distance traveled and your current location. Why? The answer is obvious.

If you know your real location and compare it with the plotted course, you immediately see the deviation from it. Which means you can correct the trajectory of movement. Thus, measurements help you correct the course and other parameters. This, in turn, guarantees arrival at the port by the scheduled time.

What happens if you deprive a ship of navigation tools? Very soon winds, currents, and storms will knock it off course. It will start circling until its resources are exhausted and the crew dies. The problem is that people do not know how to formulate measurable goals and therefore have no ability to determine the direction and speed of their movement. They, in principle, have no even slightly objective method of evaluation. That is, they are like ships whose captains supposedly want to arrive at a port, but do not know how to plot a route or correct it.

If any of you have read books on productivity, efficiency, and time management, you have noticed that they devote enormous attention to various ways of achieving goals, self-motivation, and self-organization, but, as a rule, they lack a clear approach to finding an answer to the question: "Why?" Why am I making all these efforts? What is their specific goal? In other words, what am I living for? The simplest, but the most important, question--agree.

You, my dear ones, want to master the seven habits of highly effective people, but why--most often you do not understand. Most goals are meaningless, because people invent them, forgetting the ultimate goal of their life. As a result, invented goals often turn out to be intermediate, imposed from outside, and only weakly connected to the destiny of a real person.

Our movement should be as meaningful as possible. This is an important principle. It means that first you will have to find the meaning of life, and only then will you be able to set correct goals that harmonize with your inner world. Thus, we have to get to the bottom of the meaning of life. This is important at least because, as a result, it will be very hard to throw you off. It is hard to deceive a person who knows what they want and clearly understands what they live for. Hard, but possible, if that person, while understanding themselves, does not understand the rules of how the world works.

### The Game Metaphor
To make our journey easier to perceive, I offer you a simple metaphor. Imagine that you are playing a computer game--a quest, an RPG, and an arcade all in one. It has many levels, and at each level there are its own mission, traps, puzzles, skills, artifacts, and so on. As you play, you learn, grasp its inner patterns, sharpen your skills, and try on different roles.

The goal is to complete the current level and move to a new one. There, some of your skills remain, but in changed circumstances they are no longer as effective. You are a beginner again and must acquire fundamentally different tools. At the same time, no matter what kind of player you are--skilled or not--circumstances require you to begin as quickly as possible to perceive the game world as something whole, to navigate well through all its levels, rules, and inhabitants. From any game we take away one universal lesson: the faster you manage to understand its essence, the more likely you are to reach the final level. In reality, the same principles apply. In fact, all game universes mirror our world.

Imagine that in front of you is a simplified diagram of the real world, but not in a geographic sense--in a social one. It is a crossing of two axes. On one, we mark financial position; on the other, social (public) status. Suppose that right now you are at a point where your financial and social position is insignificant. If you aim to strengthen them, you must move to the right and up on our diagram. Simple, right? Like in a computer game. I see the goal and move toward it. At the same time, I understand that different roads can lead to the same goal--a high financial and social position within the given rules.

### The Condition for Successful Completion: Continuous Learning
Each road has its pros and cons, and each, under certain conditions, can lead where you need to go. But it is important to understand: easy paths do not exist by definition. No matter what route you choose, you will encounter different creatures. They may be friendly or hostile, but all of them will have their own interests. Your task is to interact with them correctly so that they do not hinder you, but help you. With some, you can form alliances. Others you can neutralize skillfully. At the same time, you must remember: these are all temporary companions. You must be ready, sooner or later, to part ways with them.

Each participant in the Game has their own mission and their own destination point. Along the way you will pass through different territories. Everywhere there are its own laws and customs, which it is advisable to study in advance. Keep in mind that all these territories are located at different altitudes above sea level. Therefore, to get into a higher country, you will have to cross a lower one. To climb steeper and steeper, you must conserve your strength and have patience. Along the way, you will come across various traps. You must be able to recognize them and avoid them.

But in any case, in order to start moving on the map of your life, we must choose the right direction and set the main goals. We will develop a plan of movement from one intermediate goal to another so that, overall, you are moving in the right direction. When we set goals and understand that they are realistic, we will, using a metaphorical map of the world, plot a route. Some think that a path is just points connected by a line--in other words, events on the straight line of our life. In reality, this is not so. A path is a sequence of obstacles that separate us from the goal. Measuring the path in obstacles, difficulties, and uncertainty is more realistic than measuring it in distance traveled.

Once we understand what is hindering us, we will be able to define resources. That is, we will figure out what exactly needs to be studied and mastered in order to move forward successfully. We will learn to speak other languages and to behave in accordance with other customs. Because when you follow the chosen road, you must constantly attract friends and find compromises with enemies. The one who enters into dialogue rather than into battle is more likely to make it.

Everywhere there are obstacles, and to overcome them you need a whole arsenal of skills and tools. Moreover, they must be at hand by the time a person steps onto dangerous sections of the path. Otherwise, circumstances may throw them back several stages, which threatens a loss of time and strength--or something worse. Therefore, all your knowledge, skills, and tools should not only lie around waiting for a fateful showdown, but also be constantly used in training. Skills must be honed daily; otherwise they turn into meaningless junk.

Another important task is to free the mind from myths and false beliefs that have been carefully and methodically implanted in us throughout our lives. They limit your ideas about the world and your place in it, and therefore block movement across the playing field.

Thus, for success it is necessary to do three things at the same time:
- move along the path that corresponds to your global life goal;
- learn--acquire new skills and knowledge without which forward movement is impossible;
- transform yourself--build character, develop abilities, free yourself from stereotypes that reduce our effectiveness in the long and short term.

In other words, we will have to create a new personality with new values, skills, and a new worldview.

### How Path, Skills, and Personality Are Connected
First and foremost is understanding the meaning of life and the vision of a global goal. This sets the vector and motivation for movement, which means the ability to walk in the right direction without straying from the path. However, it is impossible to move along a planned route without resources. The key one is the ability to achieve results: to bring what you started to completion, to see opportunities where others only notice a blank wall, to not lose motivation until the goal--even an intermediate one--is reached.

That is why you need to learn. All your life. Continuously. Because the world changes; you move from level to level--all of this requires new knowledge and skills. But acquired knowledge and skills alone are not enough. Any movement toward a goal is the overcoming of obstacles, deprivation, need, the necessity to endure, to be ready to find resources and allies. All of this requires not only knowledge, but, first, iron will and firmness of character, and, second, freedom of thought, free of any prejudice.

Therefore, movement toward the ultimate goal of your life implies a complete transformation of personality. This includes cultivating the right character traits, developing abilities given by nature, creating a productive system of values, a complete rebuilding of your worldview, and developing entirely new principles of life. Only such a fundamental approach will ensure the achievement of any goals and the realization of the most ambitious projects.
